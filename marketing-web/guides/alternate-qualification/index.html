How to qualify a component alternate

A catalog match is a candidate, not an approval. Qualify an alternate against the exact design, application, supply need, and evidence required by your engineering process.

Cadivor can organize evidence and comparisons. It does not automatically qualify or approve a replacement.

A review process for alternate candidates

Use these checks to decide whether a candidate should advance to validation. Record unresolved questions instead of treating missing evidence as a match.

  1. Lock the exact identitiesConfirm the current and candidate manufacturer part numbers, manufacturer names, suffixes, package, and revision. Similar MPNs or typed manufacturer names alone do not establish identity.
  2. Compare electrical limits and operating conditionsUse current manufacturer datasheets. Compare the parameters that matter in this design, including supply range, absolute maximum ratings, I/O levels, timing, accuracy, drive capability, and load conditions.
  3. Check package, pinout, thermal, and environmental constraintsConfirm footprint and pin compatibility, dimensions, thermal behavior, operating-temperature range, and any mechanical or environmental requirements.
  4. Assess application behaviorIdentify firmware, startup, stability, calibration, protection, or system interactions that a catalog comparison cannot establish. Define bench and system tests appropriate to the product.
  5. Review supply evidence separately from technical fitCheck authorized-source identity, lifecycle status, stock quantity, lead time, and retrieval date. Availability does not prove compatibility, and compatibility does not prove supply coverage.
  6. Record the qualification state and ownerKeep candidate, under evaluation, approved, and rejected states distinct. Attach evidence, test results, rationale, responsible reviewer, approver, and any conditions before changing the controlled BOM.
  7. Revisit the decision when evidence changesSet a follow-up or monitoring rule only for signals that matter to the approved mitigation, such as lifecycle, stock, or lead-time changes from an authorized source.

Keep the evidence and decision together.

Compare exact parts, record the human review, and preserve open qualification work.
