A practical first rollout

Use the sequence below to make the workflow reviewable and to find data or ownership gaps early.

  1. Select a contained pilotChoose one non-sensitive BOM with a clear engineering owner and a known review date. Use a copy with the columns needed to identify parts and quantities.
  2. Agree on BOM ownership and namingDecide how projects, BOM names, revisions, and reviewers will be represented so an analysis can be found and reopened later.
  3. Validate the uploaded dataReview column mapping, part identity, manufacturer, quantity, and duplicate rows before relying on the analysis. Correct source data rather than hiding uncertain values.
  4. Review evidence with its limitsSeparate manufacturer evidence from distributor data, estimates, and missing coverage. Check exact part identity and retrieval time; do not treat stale, conflicting, or unavailable data as confirmation.
  5. Assign actions and decision authorityGive each release-impacting risk an owner, due date, evidence, and proposed mitigation. Keep preliminary review, hold, conditional approval, and approval distinct.
  6. Preserve the decision recordCapture the affected part and BOM revision, rationale, evidence snapshot, reviewer, approver, and unresolved follow-up. A recorded decision does not itself qualify a replacement.
  7. Define monitoring deliberatelyFor approved mitigations, specify which supplier or lifecycle signal should reopen the decision, how often it is checked, and what happens when a source fails or goes stale.
  8. Review the pilot before expandingAsk whether the team found the evidence, understood uncertainty, completed assigned work, and could reopen the decision. Fix the workflow before adding more programs.

What to measure in the pilot

Time to a reviewable BOM

Track parsing and correction effort, including missing, ambiguous, or duplicated fields.

Evidence coverage

Count how many prioritized parts have exact identity and source-backed evidence, and record where coverage is missing.

Decision ownership

Check whether every action has a responsible owner, due date, rationale, and the right approval state.

Follow-up closure

Review whether mitigations and monitoring rules are explicit and whether changed evidence reaches the owner.
